# Neo Kanto Encounter Tables

*All 37 locations (26 routes + 11 dungeons). Table format and mechanics match* Calli_Encounter_Tables.md *exactly.*

## How Encounters Work

Roll 2d6. Any die showing above the current threshold gets rerolled (that die only). Threshold scales with badges earned:

| Badges | Threshold | Dice show | Max possible sum | Most common sum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 2 | 1–2 | 4 | 3 |
| 1–2 | 3 | 1–3 | 6 | 4 |
| 3–4 | 4 | 1–4 | 8 | 5 |
| 5–6 | 5 | 1–5 | 10 | 6 |
| 7–8 | 6 | 1–6 | 12 | 7 |

At full threshold (7–8 badges), exact odds: 7 (16.7%) &gt; 6/8 (13.9% each) &gt; 5/9 (11.1%) &gt; 4/10 (8.3%) &gt; 3/11 (5.6%) &gt; 2/12 (2.8%, tied for rarest).

**Design note — early game reads differently than the full curve.** Only low sums are reachable before badges are earned, and within that truncated range the *middle* is what's actually common: sum 3 is the single most likely roll at 0 badges, sum 4 at 1–2 badges, sum 5 at 3–4 badges, and so on. So slots 2–4 aren't just "the low end of the eventual full curve" — for most of a low-badge player's time with a location, they *are* the entire experience. Every table below leans on that: slots 2–4 get the location's genuinely representative, well-chosen common finds, not filler.

**Slot design, independent of roll probability:** low slot number → common/base-stage species; high slot number → rare/evolved species; slot 12 is always the location's single signature rare find (a Legendary/Mythical if one is tied there, otherwise the best non-legendary "trophy" catch). This is a species-tier ordering by slot number, not a claim about how often that slot gets rolled — slot 2 and slot 12 are equally rare *rolls* (both 2.8% at full threshold), but slot 2 still holds a common/weak species and slot 12 the location's best find, because low-badge play can only ever reach slot 2–4 and shouldn't hand out rare finds there.

**Uniqueness policy:** every table below aims for 11 genuinely distinct species. Zones with a large dex pool hit this easily; a few locations pull light spillover from an immediately-adjacent zone to get there without feeling geographically dishonest (noted per-table where it happens). The one exception is Icefall Pass, a short, narrow, brand-new dungeon with only 3 native species — forcing 11 unique picks there would mean importing thematically-mismatched wildlife just to hit a number, so it repeats its native trio instead, which the ruleset explicitly endorses for low-diversity locations.

**Single-stage species and slot ordering:** a handful of species (Ditto, Absol, Lapras, Tauros, and other true one-stage "oddities") never evolve at all, so they aren't "weaker versions of themselves" the way an unevolved-but-evolvable Pokémon is. Where one of these sits at a high slot above another line's mid-evolved form, that's a deliberate read of the ruleset's stage-ordering rule as being about evolution *progression* (don't make a rare slot hand out something objectively unfinished while a common slot already has something evolved) rather than a blanket ban on one-stage species appearing anywhere but the bottom. Flagging this explicitly as a judgment call, not a hidden rule-bend.

## Design Notes

- **Route 1 vs. Viridian Forest/Route 2** share almost nothing on purpose (open farmland vs. bug/electric forest), so the two starting-area locations stay distinct.
- **Route 3 / Mt. Moon / Route 4** intentionally overlap heavily — they're one continuous rocky corridor in the fiction, so sharing most of a species pool is more honest than forcing artificial variety. Each still gets its own signature-flavored 12-slot and a different internal ordering.
- **Cerulean Cave** is the first location where the Dark/Dragon migrant wave (Sableye, Absol, Bagon/Deino lines) shows up in full, capped by Mewtwo — matches its dex/dossier description as the region's first real "deep, dangerous" location.
- **Kabutops at Mt. Moon** and **Bagon/Absol previewed at Route 26** are both deliberate cross-location appearances — a species isn't exclusive to the one zone it's filed under in the numbered dex; that filing is organizational, not a habitat restriction.
- **Route 12/13/14/15/17** lean on the Nidoran and Hitmon/Mankey/Koffing families as connective tissue across the whole southeastern stretch, with each route's *own* signature slot pulling from a different corner of the Fuchsia pool (Scyther, Nidoqueen, Nidoking, Tauros, Bellossom) so the long corridor doesn't feel like one undifferentiated table repeated five times.
- **Route 19/20 and Seafoam** share a heavy Water overlap (expected — it's one continuous sea route with an island dungeon in the middle), differentiated mainly by their signature slots: Relicanth, then Latios, then Articuno, escalating toward Cinnabar.
- **Genesect at Cinnabar Mansion** is the one location where the sum-12 slot isn't simply "roll a 12" — it's gated the same way the original games gated their own postgame secrets, layered on top of the dice mechanic rather than replacing it.
- **The Dratini line's escalation** (wild → Route 23's Dragonair → Victory Road's Dragonite) is the clearest deliberate through-line in the whole table set, mirroring the badge-gated approach to the League itself.
